An interactive dashboard for exploring UK university spin-out activity: where spin-outs are founded, who's funding them, and how that connects back to each institution's own research income, built on real HESA, UKRI, and Companies House data rather than survey estimates.

What it covers

The dashboard sits on top of a DuckDB data warehouse built from raw HESA and UKRI downloads, with a separate pipeline enriching the HESA spin-out register itself using Companies House filings and web search, since the register alone doesn't say much about what a given spin-out actually does or how it's funded. A FastAPI backend serves live queries over that warehouse (research council income, institution income and expenditure, a chart assistant) alongside a set of trained models: a funding-prediction model and a survival model, both used to compare an institution's actual research council income against what its size and profile alone would predict. The frontend (React 19 + Vite + TypeScript) turns that into the interactive dashboard: per-institution funding tables, per-research-council predictability charts, and drill-down explanations of individual figures.

Every one of the 70 institutions in scope, University of Liverpool included, gets its own drill-down report generated live from the warehouse rather than as a static document: income and expenditure trends over time, predicted-versus-actual research council funding, and spin-out activity attributed back to that institution specifically.

Enriching the spin-out register itself

HESA's own spin-out register records that a company exists and which institution it came from, and not much else, no sector, no funding history, no exit status. A separate enrichment pipeline fills that in for roughly 2,500 companies: each one is resolved to a Companies House company number, then a set of enrichment agents append what they find, sector classification, investment events (both a full historical backfill and an ongoing incremental diff), and exit signals (acquisition, dissolution, and so on), to an append-only SQLite fact store, corroborating with web search where Companies House filings alone don't settle a question. Nothing is overwritten, so the provenance of any given fact stays inspectable; the latest fact per company is then joined back against the original register and exported into the warehouse, and from there into the static JSON the frontend actually bundles.

Headline results

  • A reproducible data pipeline (raw HESA/UKRI downloads → DuckDB warehouse → both a live query API and static frontend datasets) rather than a one-off spreadsheet analysis.
  • A funding-prediction model compared against actual research council income across all 70 institutions in scope, surfacing which institutions are notably over- or under-funded relative to their predicted profile.
  • Per-council predictability varies substantially: EPSRC and MRC income are well explained by institutional size/profile (R² of 0.84 and 0.91 respectively), while STFC funding is effectively unpredictable from those features alone (R² of −0.18), consistent with STFC funding being concentrated in a small number of specialist institutions the model's features can't see.
  • The HESA spin-out register enrichment pipeline (Companies House + web search) adds the "what is this company and how is it funded" context the raw register doesn't provide on its own.
